# Zindex

Immutable agent diagrams. Every saved version is kept exactly as it was, with its own revision number and time, so a diagram made today can be read, changed and rendered the same way later, from any MCP client or the REST API.

If no `diagram_*` tools are available, Zindex isn't connected. Tell the person how to connect it:

- **They added the Zindex plugin, in an app that has Customize → Plugins:** adding the plugin doesn't connect Zindex. The person opens the Zindex plugin under Customize → Plugins, on the web or in the desktop app. On its Connectors tab, they add Zindex and connect it. If they can't add it, an owner of their organization in that app adds it first, then they connect it.
- **Anything else:** the steps for each app are at https://zindex.ai/connect/.

If you can't tell which case applies, give the person both, and say the first is for an app that has Customize → Plugins.

## The loop

1. **Find, then create.** Look for the diagram before making one: the id in the repository's `.zindex.json` if it has one, else `diagram_find` with a few words of its subject. It matches titles, keys and briefs, lists title matches first, and shows each diagram's family and the first line of its brief, so you can tell similar ones apart without opening them. Edit the one the person means rather than create a copy; create one when they want another diagram. `diagram_create` saves a new one from a DSP scene, or from Mermaid text (`erDiagram`, `flowchart` or `sequenceDiagram`), whose node IDs become the element IDs. Give it a `key` such as `payments-architecture` (repeating the create returns the same diagram instead of a copy) and a brief, which is what the next agent's search matches and reads first.
2. **Read.** `diagram_get` returns a compact outline: a header, the brief, the last three changes, then one element per line. Pass `knownRevision` with the revision you last saw to get only what changed since. `view: "mermaid"` returns the diagram as Mermaid instead.
3. **Change.** `diagram_edit` with `baseRevision` (the revision you last read), a one-line `message` and `ops`, or `mermaid`: the whole diagram as Mermaid, edited from `view: "mermaid"`. Zindex applies only the differences, so unchanged elements keep their IDs. In a sequence diagram, `after` or `before` on a create or update op puts a participant, message or note between two others. On `REVISION_CONFLICT` the error lists what changed; re-plan against the new revision. A `requestId` makes a retry after a timeout return the first result instead of applying twice. Any call that draws can answer `BUSY`: wait `retryAfter` seconds and send it again. Any tool call can answer `RATE_LIMITED` after a long burst of calls: it did nothing, so wait `retryAfter` seconds and make it again. `DRAW_TIMEOUT` means a save or edit saved nothing, so don't resend the same scene: do what the `error` says (draw fewer edges into one node, bring the elements closer together, or split the diagram). `STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE` and `STORAGE_HOLD` come only from a workspace that keeps its diagrams in its own GitHub repository; Zindex's own storage, the default, never returns them. `STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE` means the repository didn't answer: wait `retryAfter` seconds, then retry. `STORAGE_HOLD` means its connection needs a workspace owner: don't retry, and tell the person a workspace owner acts in Settings → Storage.
4. **Show.** `diagram_render` returns links.
   - Give the person the SVG link: a published diagram's links don't expire but stop if it is made private or deleted (`url` is the version's page); anything else's work for 7 days, so for a README, issue or doc that stays up, publish or make a share link (the person confirms) and see https://zindex.ai/docs/embedding/.
   - To show a diagram full screen, say on a call, the person opens `https://zindex.ai/dashboard/diagram/?id=<id>&present` while signed in (add `&revision=<n>` for one version); anyone with a version link presents it without signing in at its page plus `?present`, such as `https://zindex.ai/d/<ref>@<n>?present`.
   - To put a diagram in a page or file you publish (an artifact, slides, a PDF), make it in Zindex and show one version; such pages can't load outside images. Where you can run a command, download the version's `png` and embed it, with a command rather than typed, captioned with `zindex.ai/d/<ref>@<n>` (a published diagram's ID, otherwise a share link's token; for a private diagram with no link, its dashboard address, `https://zindex.ai/dashboard/diagram/?id=<id>&revision=<n>`, which opens for its workspace). A private diagram's copy goes only to people who may see it.
   - Where you can't run a command, the page loads the picture through the Zindex connector when it opens: `diagram_render` with `<ref>@<n>` as `id` and `preview: true` returns the PNG and its `width` and `height`, from any workspace; `<ref>` alone follows the latest version, and `knownRevision` answers `unchanged`, with no image, until a newer version is saved. Each viewer's call goes through their own connector, signed in to Zindex, so give each live section the version's page link as its fallback; anyone who can open the page can read the share link in it. A revoked link, or a diagram made private or deleted, answers `NOT_FOUND`. Recipes, and who sees a live picture: https://zindex.ai/docs/embedding/.
   - Otherwise, ask for `preview: true` only when you need to look at the picture yourself. Never hand-edit a rendered SVG or PNG: change the diagram with `diagram_edit` and render again, or the change is in no version.
   - A saved version's image prints its address in the bottom-right corner, `zindex.ai/d/<id>@<revision> · <date>`. Older images print `<id> | Rev N - <date>`. Shown an image with either line, call `diagram_get` with its id and `revision` to open the version it shows; a diagram in another workspace answers `NOT_FOUND`, and a published one reads with no key at `GET /v1/public/diagrams/{id}@{revision}`. An unsaved preview has none.

## Keep context with the diagram

- **Brief:** up to 1,000 characters on what the diagram shows, the conventions to keep and the decisions made. Start it with one line on what it shows: that line is what `diagram_find` shows. Read it before editing, and update it with `diagram_edit` (`brief`) when a convention changes.
- **Message:** one line per edit saying why.
- **Pointer file:** a code repository can keep `.zindex.json` at the root, for example `{ "diagrams": { "architecture": { "id": "dgm_7Q2kD9a1B2c3", "revision": 12 } } }`. When it's there, read it at the start of a session and pass the revision as `knownRevision`, and update it after each edit. Add one only when the person wants the diagram tracked with the code.
- **Handing off to a teammate's agent:** when the person wants a teammate's agent to pick it up, commit `.zindex.json` with the code. Their agent finds the diagram only if its key or sign-in acts in the team workspace that holds it; a diagram in your personal workspace answers `NOT_FOUND` to them until it is moved into the team workspace from the dashboard. When a teammate's diagram answers `NOT_FOUND` to you, your own key or sign-in may act in your personal workspace: see Workspaces below. They read the brief first, plan against the revision they read, and pass it as `baseRevision`. `diagram_history` shows who saved each version (`by`) and with which client.

## Workspaces

Each key or sign-in works in one workspace: the person's own, or a team workspace. Every tool acts on that workspace's diagrams, and everyone in a team workspace can read all of them. No tool takes a workspace: a diagram in another workspace answers `NOT_FOUND`, except that `diagram_render` reads a version link, or a published diagram's ID, from any workspace. To work in another workspace with sign-in (OAuth), the person opens that workspace from the workspace menu at the top of https://zindex.ai, then moves this connection there under Dashboard → API keys → Connected apps (Move here); it stays signed in. With an API key, they make a key in that workspace. To work in two at once, connect once for each.

`READ_ONLY` means the key or sign-in can read diagrams but not change or share them or report a problem with them. Don't retry. With `reason: "credential"`, the person connects again and chooses read and write, or gives a key with write access. With `reason: "role"`, they are a viewer, and a workspace owner has to make them a member. `SHARING_DISABLED` means the workspace's owners turned public sharing off: `publish` and `link` are refused, while `unpublish` and `unlink` still work. No tool creates workspaces, invites people, changes roles or manages billing; the person does that in the dashboard. In a team workspace, a teammate's diagram with your key answers `KEY_EXISTS` to your create: read and edit it instead.

## Writing scenes

- Declare `diagramFamily` and `layoutStrategy` at the document root, and leave `layout` off nodes. The engine positions nodes, routes edges and places labels.
- For a process with lanes, write a pool frame (`containerType: "pool"`, with `laneDirection`) whose `children` are lane frames (`containerType: "lane"`), each listing its tasks in `children`, with no `layout` anywhere: the engine stacks the lanes across the flow in that order and places each task in its lane. Add a task to a lane later with `createNode` and `frame` set to the lane's ID; `updateNode` with `frame` moves one to another lane.
- For an AWS architecture, set `diagramFamily: "architecture"` (or leave it out; no other family arranges AWS's groups), and leave `layout` off everything. The engine draws AWS's groups and lays Availability Zones out as columns and subnets as rows; the authoring guide's AWS section says what it does and doesn't do.
  - Write AWS's groups as frames with their presets, nested as AWS nests them (`containerType` `aws.cloud` > `aws.region` > `aws.vpc` > `aws.az` > `aws.subnet.public` or `aws.subnet.private`). A frame between a Region and its VPC, a VPC and its AZs, or an AZ and its subnets loses the arrangement inside it, unless it is a generic frame round all of a VPC's AZs, whole VPCs, or subnets next to each other in one AZ (a tier frame).
  - Write the services as nodes with `aws:*` icons, each listed in the frame it runs in: the internet gateway, VPN gateway or VPC endpoints and the load balancer in the VPC, NAT gateways in public subnets, regional services in the Region, global ones in AWS Cloud, users outside it.
  - Write a security group or Auto Scaling group across AZs, round subnets or what they hold, as a frame with `spans` instead of `children`. Its box is one rectangle round its targets, so span things that sit together: a node inside it that it doesn't span gets `SPAN_COVERS_NODE`.
- For a Google Cloud architecture, set `diagramFamily: "architecture"` (or leave it out; no other family arranges Google's groups), and leave `layout` off everything. The engine draws Google's cards and groups and lays zones out as columns and tiers as rows; the authoring guide's Google Cloud section says what it does and doesn't do.
  - Write Google's groups as frames with their presets, nested `gcp.cloud` > `gcp.region` > `gcp.subnet` > `gcp.tier` or `gcp.instanceGroup`, with an optional `gcp.project` round the Regions. Write the VPC and each zone as frames with `spans`: the VPC round its subnets, a zone round its cards in one Region.
  - Write the services as nodes with `gcp:*` icons, each listed in the frame it runs in: instances and databases in tiers, regional services in the Region, global ones in `gcp.cloud`, users outside it. Only a tier's cards stand in zone columns.
- Nodes keep their positions after the first save unless a node that grows needs the room: it pushes the nodes in its way (not in sequence diagrams), and the edit's `changed` lists them. A new node whose edge label needs more room than the column it joins pushes that column, and everything past it along the flow, just far enough, also listed in `changed`. A node an edit inserts in a flowchart, between two placed nodes, goes on the first one's line, and what lies past it moves along the flow, also listed in `changed`. A new node on a decision's no exit goes beside the decision instead. In a flowchart with a frame that has no `layout`, new nodes are placed as in other families. A new task in a lane takes a free place in its column, or the lane grows and what lies past it moves across the flow; a task inserted between two placed columns of a pool opens a column, and what lies past it moves along the flow; a task moved to another lane is placed again there. A new node in an AWS diagram takes a free place in its row or column, or the row or column grows and what lies past it moves. A row or column also grows for a spanning frame's header an edit adds, or for the room round a tier frame, and an edit that leaves a labeled line into the Region's column too little room moves the column right. A new card in a Google Cloud diagram stands in the column of the zone whose `spans` lists it: send `updateFrame` with that zone's whole `spans` in the same edit. Without a zone it goes last in its row. A new tier, Region or row item goes in at its place. A new line from a load balancer into a second Region, or from a tier past another tier, opens a gap, moving what lies past it. The load balancer moves to stay over the gap between the Regions, and the items joined only to it in its row or outside the boundary move with it where their row has room. In a Google Cloud diagram, a saved item in a row moves aside from a new line that would cross it; below a Region's subnets it does so only where the row has room, else send `autoLayout`. Except in org chart, network and sequence diagrams, a new node in a frame without a `layout` that has no room there grows the frame, and what lies past it moves across the flow. All of these are listed in `changed` too. An edit that adds or deletes a frame around placed nodes also places them again, listed in `changed`, when keeping them would leave frames overlapping or a frame holding a node that isn't its member. An `order` constraint an edit adds places its two nodes again, in order, listed in `changed`, when they stand the wrong way around. New nodes are placed next to their neighbors; edges and frames are redrawn each time; sequence diagrams follow message order. Send `autoLayout` to lay out again: `{ "op": "autoLayout" }` for every node, or with `elements` listing the nodes to place again. Laying out every node again drops a frame's own `layout` (except a pool's, a lane's or a fragment's, and in org charts and networks) and lays the frame out with its members. A frame that keeps its `layout` keeps its members where they are; name them in `elements` to place them again inside it. One that doesn't fit lands outside, reported as `NODE_OUTSIDE_FRAME`; a pool or lane grows to hold it instead.
- To number the path a request takes, give its edges `step` (1 to 99) and `stepText`, their lines in a list beside the drawing. The engine draws the numbers you give and doesn't number edges, except in a sequence diagram: its top-level `extensions.autonumber` (`true`, or `{ "start": 10, "increment": 10 }`) numbers every message from the top, and a message's own `step` restarts the count. Other families warn `AUTONUMBER_NOT_DRAWN` for it: give their edges a `step`.
- Use stable, meaningful element IDs such as `api-gateway` or `orders-db`. Ops refer to them and the history tracks them. IDs show in the SVG, as labels do.
- `diagram_create` and `diagram_edit` return `warnings` for the version they save, each with a code, the element when there is one, and a one-line fix. A diagram with warnings is saved; fix them with an edit. A write with errors saves nothing and returns `VALIDATION_FAILED` with `errors`. That includes an op on something it can't change, such as `moveElement` on an edge or `resizeElement` on a node the engine hasn't placed yet, and an edit whose result would break a rule the diagram didn't. Fix what each error names and resend with the same `baseRevision`. Within one edit, an op may name an element a later op creates, except the element `after` or `before` names and the frame a `createNode` or `updateNode` `frame` names, which must exist when the op runs. A saved edit's warnings also note what it changed besides what its ops named, such as the constraints a `deleteElement` removed. `diagram_validate` checks a draft without saving. When the docs and the engine disagree, or something you needed is missing, say so; if the person wants Zindex told, `diagram_share` with `action: "feedback"` sends it: no diagram is needed, and they confirm it first. Every code: https://zindex.ai/docs/reference/validation-rules/
- The authoring guide, [authoring.md](authoring.md) next to this file, covers element kinds, families, constraints, styles and icons. It is also at https://zindex.ai/skill/authoring.md
- Schemas: https://zindex.ai/schemas/scene.schema.json and https://zindex.ai/schemas/ops.schema.json

## Going back

- **Any version:** `diagram_get` with `revision`, or `asOf` (a date such as `2026-09-26`, meaning the end of that day in UTC, or an ISO timestamp with an offset).
- **What changed:** `diagram_history` lists versions with time, who saved each (`by`), client and message; `compare: { from, to }` lists the elements added, changed and removed between two versions or dates. A published diagram's or share link's image for one version takes `?from=<revision>` and colors what changed since, with no key (`https://zindex.ai/d/<ref>@<n>.svg?from=<m>`).
- **Undo:** `diagram_edit` with `restoreRevision` makes a new revision equal to an old one. History is never rewritten.
- **Check nothing changed:** writes, renders and `diagram_history` carry `chain`, a hash covering the version and every one before it. The same `chain` later means nothing up to it changed: https://zindex.ai/docs/concepts/#verifying

## Ask first

`diagram_delete` removes a diagram; `diagram_share` with `publish` makes it readable by anyone with its link, with `link` makes a share link that shows its images (never its scene) to anyone who has it until `unlink` revokes it, or Zindex does if the diagram breaks the content policy (on Pro and Team; on Free it returns `FEATURE_UNAVAILABLE`; publishing works on every plan and makes the scene public too), and with `report` sends one version to Zindex support (anonymized unless `anonymize` is false). The person confirms each of them. Unpublishing leaves share links working; `unlink` revokes them (what each choice shows: https://zindex.ai/docs/concepts/#sharing). Taking a link down stops new fetches at once; copies people already saved stay with them. If a tool returns `CONFIRMATION_REQUIRED`, tell the person what will happen and call again with the `confirm` token only after they agree.

## What Zindex does not do

- It renders the scene it is given. It does not draw from a picture or a description: you write the scene or the operations.
- Mermaid is accepted for erDiagram, flowchart (graph) and sequenceDiagram diagrams only. Subgraphs, edges to them and styles are left out and listed in the warnings; other Mermaid types are refused. In a sequenceDiagram only participants keep their Mermaid IDs: messages, notes, blocks, boxes, rects and bars are numbered `m1`, `note1`, `loop1`, `box1`, `rect1`, `act1` in the order written.
- A Mermaid edit changes nodes and edges, and deletes the ones you leave out. Groups, text and images keep their state. Frames do too, except in a sequence diagram, where blocks, `box` and `rect` lines change their frames and removing a `box` or `rect` deletes its frame. Add and change them with ops (createFrame, groupElements, createText, createImage). Mermaid edits work on architecture, flowchart, entity-relationship and sequence diagrams, and diagrams without a family.
- In a sequence diagram, a Mermaid edit matches messages as lines. A message whose ends change gets a new ID; to keep it, change its ends with `updateEdge`. Renaming a participant's ID makes a new participant; change its `as` label instead.
- A deleted diagram can be restored for 24 hours with `diagram_delete` and `undo: true`. After that it is gone with all its versions.
- No tool exports a diagram's whole history or the whole workspace, because a tool's result goes into your context and a whole history can run to megabytes. The person downloads one diagram's history from its page on the dashboard, and every diagram from Settings → Account (https://zindex.ai/settings/account/). With an API key: `GET https://api.zindex.ai/v1/diagrams/{id}/export` or `GET https://api.zindex.ai/v1/workspace/export` (a team workspace's owners only).
